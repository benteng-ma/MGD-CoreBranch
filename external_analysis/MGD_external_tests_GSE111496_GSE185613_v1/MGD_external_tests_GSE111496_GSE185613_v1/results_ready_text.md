# Manuscript-ready Results draft

### A targeted inflammatory-defense panel is inducible in human meibomian gland epithelial cells
In GSE111496, all 10 direct human counterparts of the pre-existing Awat2 inflammatory-defense panel were represented on the Illumina array. In the 12 HMGEC samples, LPS/LBP increased the sample-level defense score in both the DHT-naive and DHT-treated strata (stratum effects 0.329 and 0.545, respectively). The stratified average LPS/LBP effect was 0.437, with a restricted exact two-sided permutation P=0.015. The result remained positive in every leave-one-gene-out analysis (two-sided P range 0.005–0.045) and under a maximum-variance representative-probe rule (P=0.025). The DHT-by-LPS interaction was not significant (P=0.407). Gene-level effects were heterogeneous rather than uniformly positive, with the strongest average increases in CXCL1, CXCL5 and CCL20. These findings support targeted inflammatory-defense inducibility in a human MG epithelial system but do not constitute human MGD disease replication.

### The fixed defense panel does not transfer to the HFD/circadian MG-eyelid dataset
All 10 fixed mouse-panel genes were represented in GSE185613. After accounting for the three collection times, the HFD-associated module effect was negative (-0.564) and was not significant by the time-stratified exact permutation test (two-sided P=0.296). Time-specific module differences were -1.023 at 9h, 0.175 at 15h and -0.842 at 21h. Leave-one-gene-out analyses retained a negative overall HFD effect, and a percentile-rank score gave the same qualitative result (P=0.287). Thus, this metabolic/circadian dataset does not support universal transfer of the fixed inflammatory-defense panel and instead defines an external boundary of the Core–Branch framework.
