## Suggested Results text

### A previously defined inflammatory-defense panel transfers to an independent rat UVB-MG context
GSE291177 provided an independent rat meibomian-gland RNA-seq context with four untreated wild-type and three UVB-treated wild-type deposited libraries. Of the 10 genes in the previously defined Awat2 inflammatory-defense panel, nine were represented in the deposited TPM matrix; Cxcl5 was not represented. All nine measurable genes had higher mean log2(TPM+1) expression in UVB-treated than untreated wild-type libraries. The sample-level nine-gene defense score was higher in every UVB-treated wild-type library than in every untreated wild-type library (mean score difference=1.258; exact two-sided permutation P=0.0286). This result was unchanged when standardization used all 17 deposited libraries, when a percentile-rank module score was used, and in all nine leave-one-gene-out analyses (all exact two-sided permutation P=0.0286; complete group separation retained in every leave-one-out analysis). Thus, an inflammatory-defense program defined before examination of GSE291177 transferred to an etiologically distinct, cross-species UVB-MG context, while individual fixed genes should not be interpreted as a universal signature.

In the P1.hMR background, the same panel also separated UVB-treated from untreated libraries (exact two-sided permutation P=0.0079); however, a common-scale two-factor model did not support a significant UVB-by-genotype interaction for the module score (interaction P=0.416). This secondary analysis therefore supports recurrence of the UVB-associated program but does not establish that MR overexpression significantly amplifies the transferred module.
